# The standard

The screen follows the AAOIFI Shariah Standard on financial papers (shares and bonds), as applied by the main retail screeners. Where screeners differ in a detail (which market-capitalisation basis, whether to use total assets), say which basis was used and why; the thresholds below are the ones to apply.

## 1. Core-business test (first; any fail is a FAIL regardless of ratios)

Excluded as a core business, or as the unit a role or client sits in:

- conventional banking, lending and interest-based finance, including buy-now-pay-later and consumer credit;
- conventional insurance (takaful is not excluded);
- cryptocurrency exchanges and speculative trading platforms, and tokens with no underlying business;
- alcohol production or sale as a main line; tobacco and vaping; cannabis for recreation;
- gambling, betting, lotteries and gaming with wagering;
- adult content; pork and non-halal meat as a main line;
- weapons and defence as the primary business, or a unit that serves it;
- music, film and entertainment where it is the dominant business;
- anything whose core mandate serves the above (a consultancy's financial-services or defence practice, a software vendor whose main customers are casinos or lenders).

A company passes the core-business test when the excluded activities are incidental and the non-compliant revenue is below 5% of total revenue. A role or client passes when neither the company nor the specific unit and work fall in the list.

## 2. Ratios (stocks, and the holdings of a fund)

| Ratio | Threshold | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Interest and other non-compliant income / total revenue | below 5% | Latest annual, or trailing twelve months |
| Interest-bearing debt / market capitalisation | below 33% | Market cap as 12-month or 36-month average where the screener states it; say which |
| Cash and interest-bearing securities / market capitalisation | below 33% | Same basis |

Some screeners use total assets instead of market capitalisation for the debt and cash ratios; if the two bases give different verdicts, the stricter result stands and the verdict says so.

## 3. Screens by product type

| Type | Screen |
|---|---|
| Stock | Core business, then the three ratios, then two screeners agreeing. Report the interest-income percentage even when it passes. |
| Fund (ETF, index, active, multi-asset) | CLEAN only for a fund that is screened to a Shariah index or methodology stated in its documents, with a named Shariah board or index provider. A conventional fund that happens to hold compliant names is BORDERLINE at best. Any fund holding bonds, money-market instruments for yield, or conventional financials is a FAIL. Name the index or methodology and the purification policy. |
| Cash | Conventional interest is a FAIL (riba). Prize-draw savings are a FAIL (riba and gambling). CLEAN: Islamic-bank products on an expected-profit-rate basis under a Shariah board, and Shariah-compliant cash ISAs. Money-market funds are FAIL unless Shariah-screened. |
| Wrapper (ISA, pension, platform) | Neutral in itself. Screen every holding inside it and give one verdict per holding, plus one line for the wrapper: "wrapper neutral; holdings as listed". A default pension fund is usually conventional and a FAIL until changed. |
| Employer, client, role | Core-business test on the company, then on the unit and the work itself. A clean company's defence or lending unit is a FAIL for a role in it. Equity and bonus paid in the company's shares are screened as a stock. |

## 4. Purification

For a CLEAN or BORDERLINE stock, the non-compliant income must be purified:

purification per share = non-compliant income for the period / shares outstanding; amount owed = purification per share × shares held, for each period held. Where the screener publishes a purification figure, use it and cite it; otherwise compute from the filing and show the arithmetic. Dividends received from a FAIL name are purified in full for the period the name was held after it failed.

For a fund, use the fund's published purification ratio; if none is published, that is a point against CLEAN.

## 5. Holding a name that fails

The standard allows a holding period to exit a name that turns non-compliant after purchase; the usual retail reading is to sell within a reasonable period once the failure is confirmed, and to purify gains made after the failure date. The verdict quotes this when it applies, and leaves the decision to the person.

## 6. Re-screen triggers

- A new annual or quarterly filing.
- A price move large enough to change the debt or cash ratio against market capitalisation (as a rule of thumb, 25% either way).
- A screener changing its verdict.
- An acquisition, a new business line, or a change of fund index.
- For an employer, a change of unit, client or product line.
- Otherwise every six months, and always at the moment of purchase, commitment or acceptance.
