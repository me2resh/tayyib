# Fennbridge Bank Easy Saver: Tayyib verdict

Screened on 10 February 2026. Standard: AAOIFI core-business test, then the three ratios; two independent sources must agree. Sample verdict: the bank and the figures are illustrative and the links point at example.org; the method and the layout are as shipped.
Verdict: ❌ FAIL

## 1. The verdict in one paragraph

The account pays interest at 4.1% AER on a conventional banking balance sheet, which is riba, so it fails the core-business test and no ratio can rescue it ✅ [^1] [^2]. The verdict is the same for every conventional interest-bearing account regardless of rate, and it does not depend on the screeners, who agree ✅ [^3] [^4]. The alternative is an expected-profit-rate account at an Islamic bank under a Shariah board, which this verdict names in section 5.

## 2. Core business

Fennbridge Bank is a conventional retail bank; its income is net interest on lending ✅ [^2]. The product's terms state that the return is "interest, calculated daily and paid monthly" ✅ [^1]. Fail on the core-business test: the product is an interest contract.

## 3. Ratios

Not applicable: the ratios screen a company's balance sheet for a shareholder; this is a deposit contract whose return is interest by its terms ✅ [^1].

## 5. Reasoning

Interest is the return, by the product's own terms, so the verdict is FAIL and the rate is irrelevant ✅ [^1]. The deposit protection scheme and the ease of access do not change it. The compliant alternative in the same market is an easy-access account at an Islamic bank paying an expected profit rate under a published Shariah board, structured as commodity murabaha or wakala; two such banks publish rates and boards on their product pages ✅ [^5] [^6]. The expected profit rate is a target, not a promise, which is the point.

## 6. Purification

Not applicable to the product. Interest already received on a balance held here is not the holder's to keep: give it away without intending reward, and record the amount and the date ✅ [^3].

## 7. Re-screen

Not needed. The product's basis of return is stated as interest; only a change to an expected-profit structure under a Shariah board would reopen it.
